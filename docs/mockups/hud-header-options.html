E, revised · Rail that changes by goal after the walkthrough below

Two short rows instead of one rail. Top: the goal bar, drawn to suit the goal, with its words and numbers inside it, then the stars, then the menu. Bottom: the things that can lose the level or that you spend, which never leave the screen.

Why the original E fails, level by level
  1. The stars don't belong on the goal bar. Placing stars along the rail assumes they unlock as the goal fills. In the real levels they are separate things. "Beat 2:38" (L3, L4, L8 and most others) is a race against the clock. "Never runs dry" or "no angry customers" (L1, L8, L13) can only be lost, never earned midway. "20-sale streak" (L3, L40) is a counter of its own. A star drawn at 33% of the goal bar would point at nothing.
  2. Heat vanishes. Every level can be lost by the reactor running dry, but the rail only shows the main goal. On L1 the sentence mentions heat because heat is the goal. On L18 (earn 237 credits) the sentence would talk about credits, and the player would have no idea the reactor is draining until the frost hits. The same goes for wall health on defend levels (L5, L37 fail on it).
  3. Coins vanish. Nearly every level is about buying pads. Without a visible balance the player can't tell whether to keep gathering or go and spend.
  4. A plain fill bar misreads most goals. It works for counts (L4: 12 of 23 meals, L17: hunt 27) and for survive timers (L32). It fails for:
    • Hold goals (L1 heat for 1:25, L25 automate 36 s, L31 two machines running 15 s, L35 80-sale streak). Progress resets when the condition breaks, so the bar would silently jump back to zero. It also sits empty for the whole first minute of L1 while heat climbs to 70%, telling the player nothing.
    • Few-step goals (L11, L20 reactor to level 3; L10 turret level 3; L40 unlock 2; L16 clear 1 zone; L42 build the landmark; L15 kill 1 boss). The bar moves in two or three big jumps and sits still in between, while the useful information is how close you are to affording the next step.
    • Waves (L5, L37). Steps again, and the thing to watch between waves is the countdown.
  5. 6 px at the very top edge is too thin. On an iPhone it sits beside the Dynamic Island and status bar, it can't hold a label, and it's too thin to tap for details.
  6. The sentence is often empty. Guide instructions only show on levels 1–5, or when you seem stuck. After that the line would be blank or would repeat the goal.
How the revised bar draws each kind of goal
  • Counts (earn, produce, sell, rescue, hunt, haul, hire, admit, population, meals, chains, orders, premium guests): a fill with "12/23 meals" inside it, plus a tick per unit when the target is 10 or less (rescue 4).
  • Holds (heat hold, automate, machines running, survive, earn within a time, streak): the bar names the condition and fills while it holds. When it breaks, the bar flashes and drains back, so the reset is visible instead of silent. Before the condition is first met, the bar shows how far off it is ("Heat 50% → 70%").
  • Steps (reactor, machine or turret levels, unlocks, zones, landmark, boss, waves): one segment per step. The open segment fills with credits saved toward the next purchase, with boss health, or with the countdown to the next wave.
  • Stars sit beside the bar with a state each: open (☆), holding (an outline that can still be lost), earned (gold ★), lost (struck through). The time star shows its countdown in place of the icon.
  • Vitals under the bar: coins always, heat always, wall health on defend levels, each with its star floor marked. They pulse red when near a loss. This row is where F's "quiet" idea could still apply to anything else.

F · Quiet HUD shows only what changed

Nothing stays on screen that isn't changing or at risk. A coin count slides in when you pick some up or step on a pad and fades after a few seconds. When something goes wrong, the edges of the screen frost over, so you feel the danger before you read it.

G · One live sentence plain words

≡

Heat is 50%. Get it to 70% and hold it for 1:25.

A single line written for this moment and rewritten as things change: "Hold it: 0:41 left", "Heat is falling, feed the reactor". It reads like a teammate talking, and it can absorb the guide arrow's instruction too, so there is one place to look for what to do.

Mixing them

These combine well. D's ring on the reactor and G's sentence together would cover Level 1 with no header at all, and F's quiet rule (show it only when it changes) can apply to any of A–G. Defend levels would put the wall's health on the walls and wave timing in the sentence the same way.
